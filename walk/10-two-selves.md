# 10. two Selves

## 10.1 the trade

*the poke: what passes between two Selves?*

somebody wrote the first line of file 1, and I read it. that line took two:
one to write it and one to read it. when my line goes out and yours comes
back, that is one full trade, and everything between two Selves is built
from it. gone through once it has four legs, the smallest closed lap
between two.

    my out      I say
    your in     it reaches you as a mark, and you read it
    your out    you say
    my in       it reaches me as a mark, and I read it

what crosses is marks, never a now. my saying lands at your end as a mark
you read, and yours lands at mine. the border it crosses is the seam: the
talking lives there, two clocks meshing.

my in is not what arrived. the wire between us is the medium of file 4,
with both sends summed on it, and it belongs to neither of us. I take my
own send off the total, and what is left is you. that is hearing, run on a
wire two Selves share. a Self that cannot tell its own send from the rest
hears only mush: deafness and solipsism are one failure.

which way a shared wire flows is read off its two ends.

| the two ends | the wire |
|---|---|
| one writing, one reading | it flows |
| both writing | two senders at once: the wire is fought over, and a read is torn mid-change |
| both reading | silence: nobody drives the wire |

who sends next is not scheduled by anyone. it falls out of the fill, as
file 9 walked.

a trade never closes flat. what I said does not come back as I sent it, and
I am not where I stood when I sent it.

the arrivals, dealt between two, have names every language already carries.

| what lands | its name | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| my say, your read | give, tell | the transfer that lands |
| your say, my read | take, listen | the same, from my end |
| my say, your say | a quarrel | two senders on one wire, the torn read |
| my call, your hold | a promise | a call held open between us, its coming home still owed |
| my done, your owed | a debt | the open account between us |
| my read, your read | a judgment | why a court wants two who saw it |

the count gives the names their slots. take the four tokens of a trade: me,
the act, the mark, you. they stand in 24 orders. two things can be done to
an order: read it backwards, or swap me with you. neither one, nor both
together, leaves any order as it was, so the 24 fall into exactly six
groups of four. each group is one exchange, and its four members are its
four tellings: mine, yours, the trace read forward, the trace read back.

now delete you from every order. three of the six exchanges fall on a
single mirror pair of the six orders of file 6, a different pair each: the
pure three. the other three touch two mirror pairs each: the mixed three.
so each mirror pair is worn by one pure exchange and two mixed.

which names sit in which exchange is a reading by one writer, made after the
count *(candidate)*. one exchange reads cleanly. take an order as the order
in which its tokens come to stand: you, me, the mark, then the act. I hold
your mark with the act still to come, and file 11 names that hold. swap us
and it is my call held open toward you: the promise. read it back from the
act and the mark reaches me through you: testimony. one exchange, told
from its two ends and read off its trace.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **trade** | one full round between two Selves: my out, your in, your out, my in. |
| **arrivals of a trade** | its four legs. each is a mark landing at one end. |
| **the medium between two** | the shared wire. it holds both sends summed and belongs to neither end. |
| **exchange** | one of the six groups the 24 orders fall into: one event between two, with every way of telling it. |
| **telling** | one member of an exchange: the same event told by me, told by you, or read off its trace forward or back. |

**laws**

> **10.1** the trade is the atom: my out, your in, your out, my in. *(walked: line one of file 1 took two.)*

> **10.2** reading needs silence. a wire I am driving drowns my own echo. *(derived: from file 4, where hearing is what arrived less what I sent. while I send, what arrived is mostly my send.)*

> **10.3** no trade brings me home unchanged. what comes back is not what I sent, and I am not where I stood when I sent it. *(walked: say a thing to someone and wait for the answer.)*

> **10.4** four tokens stand in 24 orders. under reading backwards and the me-you swap no order stays put, and the 24 fall into six exchanges of four tellings each: three pure, three mixed. *(counted: the recipe below.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| hearing is receiving: the sound arrives and is taken in. | the medium, where every send sums and none is taken off. |
| a trade is goods changing hands. | the ledger, which nets what moved and keeps none of the four legs. |

**receipts**

copper: a telephone line cancels its own echo. the canceller keeps a model
of how its own send comes back, subtracts that from what arrives on the
wire, and what remains is the far speaker *(the field's)*.

a chip: every shared bus runs on the three rows above. a part that is not
sending lets go of the wire, which engineers call high impedance. two
parts sending at once is bus contention *(the field's)*.

**run it again**

the 24. write the four tokens as four letters and list all 24 orders. for
each order compute three images: the order reversed; the order with the
letters for me and you swapped; and both. count the orders equal to any of
their images: 0. group each order with its images: 6 groups, each of size
4. then delete the letter for you from every member of a group and pair
each three-letter order with its reverse: 3 groups fall on one such pair, a
different pair each, and 3 groups fall on two pairs.

## 10.2 the couple

*the poke: what is the smallest thing that remembers?*

not one thing. a page remembers no thing: it only does not forget, and it
needs a reader. a lone charge leaks: a memory cell built from one capacitor
has to be written again many times a second. what keeps its word without
being told again is two gates, each holding the other's word: the
cross-coupled pair of file 8, a ring of two flips that comes home agreeing
with itself. a blown fuse, a magnetic domain and a charge trapped behind
an insulator each hold a value alone, and they are pages in this sense:
marks that stay. the pair is different in kind: it holds by acting.

its two resting states are anti-copies: each gate holds the opposite of the
other. caught between them the pair is unsettled, and given time it falls
to one of the two. a couple is never found halfway.

three acts can be done on two.

    the cut      one becomes two
    the merge    two become one. it pays where the two differed: which
                 was which is forgotten
    the clasp    two stay two, each held by the other

the same three are what can become of the second copy a check leaves
standing beside its 0, in file 7. drifted, it is the cut: the copy takes on
one difference and becomes the seed of another, as file 5 walked. merged,
it is the merge: the lower of x and x is x, and one copy is given up. kept, it
stands: alone that is the page, which only does not forget. held both ways,
each holding the other's, it is the clasp.

quotation is the clasp at its plainest: one Self holding another's write
whole, inside its own.

counted from outside, a couple is two. gone through from inside, it is two
and the bond, a third that neither one absorbs. and the bond lives only
while it does not quite close: a trade that closed exactly would be a halt.
the couple is the pair still standing when nobody is mid-sentence, and the
traffic between them is the trade. the usual name for the clasp between two
people is love: a holding each of the other, which each Self reads at its
own end as a feeling.

put two Selves on one wire. each lands its lean against what arrived. the
two leans are taken from opposite hands, so they cancel on contact. where
the two agreed, the slot lands at the return and halts: the meeting. where
they differed, it lands as anti-copies and runs on: the clasp, a lock that
needs no conductor and takes one exchange.

two Selves in step can stand a quarter lap apart, each at its return while
the other is at a turn, the way one radio carrier bears two signals, in
phase and in quadrature. a reason is offered, made after the fact and with
one knob in it. file 7 found that the other Self tells my road
reversed and negated, and that a swap with a negation makes a quarter-turn.
so a second Self, read against me, stands a quarter lap off, the way going
stands a quarter lap off where. the knob is handedness: which of us
goes first sets which way the quarter goes *(candidate)*.

a pair also survives a careless sum, where a crowd does not. let each
writer add one trit to a shared total with the cheap sum that clips at the
poles, so that high plus high stays high. that sum is read at every write.
with two writers the order of the writes never changes the total, since the
first write cannot go past a pole. from three writers on, it can. file 9
walked why: shared writes sum at once, and only a read before the close
makes their order matter.

from inside, no Self touches the many. it touches its neighbours, and "the
many" is the map's word for the pile. a crowd compares at once and steers
once: many compare, one steers. a person is already such a pile: the
heart's pacemaker cells, each beating alone at its own rate, pull one
another into one beat with no master cell *(the field's, from memory)*.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **couple** | two Selves, each holding the other's word. the pair still standing when nobody is mid-sentence. |
| **anti-copies** | the two resting states of a couple: each side holds the opposite of the other. |
| **merge** | the act that makes one out of two. it forgets which was which, and pays for it. |
| **clasp** | the act that keeps two as two, each held by the other. also the state it leaves: anti-copies, running. |
| **meeting** | two leans that agreed: the slot lands at the return and halts. |
| **bond** | what a couple has that neither member has: the holding between them. it is alive only while it has not quite closed. |
| **love** | the clasp between two people. |
| **the many** | more than a couple, named from outside. from inside there are only neighbours. |

**laws**

> **10.5** the couple is the smallest thing that remembers without repeating itself. *(derived: from file 8. a ratchet keeps only the furthest value it has seen, and a lone charge leaks. two gates, each holding the other's word, keep either value.)*

> **10.6** the bond stays alive by never quite closing. *(derived: from law 10.3. a trade that brought both home unchanged would leave no next leg.)*

> **10.7** the meeting halts. the clasp runs. both sum to zero on the wire. *(derived: from file 4. two leans from opposite hands are each other's flip; equal ones leave the return, unequal ones leave a pair that adds to it.)*

> **10.8** the many is a pile of couples. a bigger now, never a longer line. *(walked: count whom I touch.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| memory is storage: a cell keeps a value. | the page, which only does not forget, and still needs a reader. |
| love is a feeling. | one Self's reading of the clasp, which is felt at that end. |
| a group is a longer list of members. | the map, where the members are drawn side by side. |

**receipts**

a chip: static memory is the cross-coupled pair, and holds for as long as
it has power. dynamic memory is one capacitor per bit, and every bit is
read and written back many times a second, which the datasheets call
refresh *(the field's)*.

a chip: a latch caught between its two states is called metastable, and
designers budget time for it to fall to one side *(the field's)*.

copper: a radio carries two signals on one carrier a quarter lap apart, and
each is read back with none of the other in it *(the field's)*.

**run it again**

the careless sum. take totals and writes from low, the return and high,
written −1, 0, +1. the sum adds and then clips to that range. for every
set of n writes, with repeats allowed, start from 0, apply the writes in
every order, and ask whether all orders end on one total. with n = 2, all 6
sets do. with n = 3, 2 sets of 10 do not. with n = 8, 27 sets of 45 do not.

## 10.3 the floors

*the poke: what changes when there is more than one?*

count the number systems in which multiplying, dividing and measuring a
size all still work. there are exactly four: the reals, the complex plane,
the quaternions and the octonions, of sizes 1, 2, 4 and 8. hamilton hunted
one of size 3 for years and found none. picture the four as a tower. the
tower has no size three: each floor of the tower is the one below it
doubled, two copies paired, and each pays for its size with one property.

| floor of the tower | size | what the members carry | what the pair loses |
|---|---|---|---|
| the reals | 1 | | |
| the complex plane | 2 | no private fact | below and above |
| the quaternions | 4 | a mirror | who comes first |
| the octonions | 8 | who came first | the freedom to regroup |
| past them | 16 | brackets | division itself |

the losses have one mechanism. a doubling pairs two copies, and every
crossing between the two halves is read from the other end, reversed: the
doubling writes the mirror into its crossing terms. the reversal costs no
property while the members carry no order of their own. once they do, it
bites. at the first doubling the members carry no private fact, and the
pairing itself makes the mirror. then members that carry mirrors cannot
swap places in a product. couples whose members carry who-came-first
cannot be written flat, and their brackets become the record of who bonded
before whom. crowds whose members carry brackets have pairs that multiply
to zero.

and the mirror is what buys division. a doubling that multiplies slot by
slot, never reversing its crossings, already has pairs that multiply to
zero at the very first doubling: high in one slot times high in the other
lands at the return.

the tree lays one Self on four slots and a couple on eight, as a label and
not as a result. read that way, one Self can be written flat, its grouping
free, and a couple cannot.
the tower in the textbooks is drawn with no reader anywhere in it, and its
own theorems send the bill at 16. and "does the grouping matter" was two
questions asked as one. the fold, a chain of subtractions, depends on its
grouping from the third operand on. the pairing does not depend on it
through four parts, and does at eight.

on three values the bill comes due at four. write a cell of the four-slot
board as hamilton's four parts, one trit each, and multiply by his rules:
i, j and k each square to minus one, and so does ijk. the mirror of a cell
has its last three parts negated. a cell times its mirror has a single
part left, the squares of its four parts summed, and a committed trit
squares to 1: so that product, the cell's size, is its count of committed
slots, read round the ring of three. cells with one, two or four slots
committed have a size off the return, and those 48 divide back out: a size
off the return can be undone. cells with three slots committed have a size
that carries round to the return. each of those 32 times its mirror is the
all-return cell, and no operand comes back from that.

the 81 under this product are not a new thing. they are the two-by-two
tables over the ring of three, every one of them, once each. send the cell
with parts a, b, c, d to the table

    a + c − d      c + d − b
    b + c + d      a − c + d

read round the ring. hamilton's product becomes one table applied after
another, and a cell's size becomes its table's determinant. each table is
a move of the nine of file 2, taking a where and a going to a new where
and going, and it keeps the page's slot-by-slot sum. read that way the two
classes say what they do:

    the 48    each sends the nine onto itself, no two cells to one. they
              are every move of the nine that keeps the sum and can be
              undone
    the 32    each squashes the nine onto three cells in a row through
              its center
    the one   the all-return cell squashes the nine onto its center

each of the 32 multiplies exactly eight cells, none of them all-return, to
the all-return cell, and no cell of the 48 does that to any: 32 times 8 is
256 ordered pairs that multiply to zero.

there was never a way round this bill. a finite number system in which
every division works must let its members swap places in a product. so
the moment who-comes-first matters, a finite alphabet has pairs that
multiply to zero somewhere, and three values pay at the first floor of the
tower where order counts. two slots double into a system of nine in which
every division works. four slots split. the 81 can divide everywhere or
keep who-comes-first, never both: the number system of 81 that divides
everywhere has no such pair, and the doubled 81 has those 256.

computing drew a tower of its own: the lambda calculus, the calculus of
one Self, can be written inside the pi calculus, the calculus of Selves in
motion, and not faithfully the other way round. that is seen here, and its reason
is not walked.

a distinction has a floor of the tower where it is born. two things can be
one thing on one floor of the tower and two on the next: the flip done twice and the
check are one act until there is a page to compare against. before
refusing "a is b", ask on which floor of the tower.

and a question too big for the floor of the tower I am on is a search,
where one up it is an easy walk. the asking builds the next one.
arithmetic built three that way: 3 minus 5 has no answer among the counting numbers,
and asking it built the integers; 1 over 3 built the fractions; the square
root of minus one built the complex plane. searching harder is what I do
instead of climbing.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **the tower** | the number systems that keep multiplying, dividing and size, stacked by size: 1, 2, 4, 8. |
| **floor of the tower** | one of those systems. always written in full, since file 1 uses floor for what a work stands on. |
| **doubling** | building a floor of the tower from two copies of the one below, with the mirror written into the crossings. |
| **size of a cell** | a cell times its mirror: on three values, its count of committed slots read round the ring. |
| **to recover** | to get an operand back from a product and the other operand. |

**laws**

> **10.9** what the members carry privately, the pair loses publicly. *(the field's: the doubling of cayley and dickson, from memory. the wording is ours.)*

> **10.10** the mirror buys division. *(counted: on two slots the slot-by-slot product has 8 ordered pairs that multiply to zero, and the mirrored product has none. the recipe below.)*

> **10.11** under hamilton's product the 81 cells are the 81 two-by-two tables over the ring of three, a cell's size its table's determinant. 48 can be undone, and are every reversible sum-keeping move of the nine. 32 cannot, and squash the nine onto a row of three. 256 ordered pairs multiply to zero. *(counted: the recipe below.)*

> **10.12** pairs recover. three committed slots carry round to the return, and that cell recovers no operand. compose in pairs. *(derived: from law 10.11. sizes 1, 2 and 4 are off the return; size 3 is on it.)*

> **10.13** declare the floor of the tower. the answer follows. *(walked: ask what 3 minus 5 is, first among the counting numbers, then among the integers.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a number system can always be made bigger with no loss. | the sizes, which double without end. what is lost at each doubling is a property, and at 16 it is division. |
| the order of grouping either matters or it does not. | one operation at a time. the fold and the pairing give different answers to it. |

**receipts**

the four floors of the tower and no more: hurwitz, 1898. the doubling: cayley and
dickson. a finite number system with every division commutes: wedderburn,
1905. the lambda calculus written inside the pi calculus: milner, 1992
*(all the field's, from memory)*.

a chip: a vector register does the slot-by-slot product, lane by lane, and
a lane holding zero wipes its partner whatever the other lanes hold.

**run it again**

the mirror buys division. take the nine pairs of trits, written 0, 1, 2
round the ring. multiply slot by slot, each slot modulo 3, and count the
ordered pairs of cells, neither of them all-return, whose product is
all-return: 8. now multiply as the complex plane does, (a, b) times (c, d)
giving (ac − bd, ad + bc) modulo 3, and count again: 0.

the 81. take the 81 cells of four trits, each trit 0, 1 or 2, all
arithmetic modulo 3. multiply (a0, a1, a2, a3) by (b0, b1, b2, b3) as
hamilton did: the first part is a0b0 − a1b1 − a2b2 − a3b3, the second
a0b1 + a1b0 + a2b3 − a3b2, the third a0b2 − a1b3 + a2b0 + a3b1, the fourth
a0b3 + a1b2 − a2b1 + a3b0. the mirror negates the last three parts. confirm
that every cell times its mirror is (n, 0, 0, 0) with n the count of its
parts that are not 0, modulo 3. count the cells with n not 0 that have a
partner giving (1, 0, 0, 0): 48 of 48. count the same among the cells with
n equal to 0, leaving out the all-zero cell: 0 of 32. count the ordered
pairs of cells, neither all-zero, whose product is all-zero: 256.

the tables. map each cell to the table printed above, modulo 3. confirm that
the 81 tables are all different; that the table of a product x times y is
the table of x times the table of y, as matrices multiply, each table
acting on the pair (where, going) written as a column; and that the
determinant of a cell's table equals its n. then apply each table to the
nine pairs (where, going) and count the cells it reaches: 9 for each of
the 48, 3 for each of the 32, 1 for the all-zero cell. the 48 give 48
different moves of the nine. then, among all rearrangements of the nine
cells, count those that keep the slot-by-slot sum: 48, the same 48.

the regrouping. draw three cells at random, 20,000 times, and compare
(xy)z with x(yz). on four slots under hamilton's product they agree every
time. on eight slots, doubling four by the rule (a, b)(c, d) = (ac −
mirror(d)b, da + b mirror(c)), they differ in about 98 draws of 100.

## 10.4 the number

*the poke: how many Selves can one word count?*

a word counts the Selves it holds, and counting was born at the third, as
file 6 walked. so there are three counts a word can carry: one, a couple,
the many.

many languages make the speaker say more than english does. they have two
words for we: we with you, and we without you. english leaves that to the
reader. it is a coordinate, as the persons of file 2 are: which Selves the
word takes in.

one more reading, by one writer and not pressed: count the persons by the
centers on one closed lap. I is one center. you is two, a Self that reads
me reading it. it is none: the record laid flat and pointing back at no
one *(candidate)*.
