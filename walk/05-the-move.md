# 5. the move

## 5.1 the three offices

*the poke: when I make a move, who pays, what was on offer, and where does it land?*

every move has three parts, and the period test of section 1.2 already had all
three.

    the one who pays      picks, and pays for the pick
    what is on offer      every option held ready, none landed
    where it lands        every pick so far, landed and standing

before the press, every key on the keyboard was on offer. my press paid.
the page took the period, and it stands there for anyone to read.

these three are offices, not things. an office is a job in the move, named
by what it does, and it is filled now by one thing and now by another. two
players at one board show it. while the move is mine I am in the paying
office: the legal moves are on offer to me, and the board takes the one I
make. then the move is yours, and the paying office is yours. the pieces
did not change. the offices were dealt again. and what fills one office
for me fills another for you: the position I landed is what your options
are read from. the three offices are forced. what fills each is free.

the paying office has a name here: the source. it is a word to watch. I
say it and watch where my attention goes. if it goes to something I can
point at, the finger that pressed the key, the Self was just written as a
mark. I can point at the finger: it is a thing I see, and what I see of it
has already landed. it is on my page. the paying is not the finger. by law 1.4
the one who lands the marks is never landed, so the one who pays is met
only in the paying.

from inside, the source is whoever pays for the move happening now. one
payer placed outside every page, paying for everybody's moves, would be
the decree of section 1.5 again: a floor nobody can walk to.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **office** | one of the three jobs in a move: paying, being on offer, taking the landing. an office is a job, and what fills it changes. |
| **source** | the paying office: whoever picks and pays for the move happening now. |

**laws**

> **5.1** whatever I can point at is on my page. the one who pays never is. *(derived: from laws 1.2 and 1.4. what can be pointed at has landed, and the one who lands is never landed.)*

> **5.2** the source is an office, not an address. the word I belongs to whoever pays now. *(walked: two players at one board.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| whoever acts is a thing that can be pointed at: a person, a machine. | the actor's visible parts, which are on the page of whoever points. |

**receipts**

a chip: one write to memory has the three. the instruction that issues it
pays, every address it could have named was on offer, and one cell takes
the value. the same cell is where one instruction lands and what the next
one reads from: what fills an office depends on who reads it.

## 5.2 the menu

*the poke: what holds every option and lands none?*

every option I could take is already written. the branches on offer stand
whole before I look. what no page holds is which one I take next, and that
is mine. the office of being on offer has a name here: the menu.

the old word for the menu is fate. the usual reading hears in it the picks
already made, and half of that is right: every branch is written. the
picks are not.

looking over a menu is free. by file 4 a look lands no mark, so I can go
over every option a hundred times and the page is as it was. I do not
visit the options I look over. I stay where I stand, at the chair, and
they are all measured from there. and the menu itself lands no mark
either: holding options ready changes no page. only the pick pays, once,
when it lands.

how much does a menu hold? one trit holds three options: below the return,
on it, or above it. each trit more multiplies by three: 3, 9, 27, 81. at
two trits, four of the nine cells have both trits at a pole, and five
still hold a return. take away the cells with every trit at a pole and
what is left is the held middle: 1, 5, 19 and 65 cells at one, two, three
and four trits. a return in a cell is a reach unspent: one option still
held. the cells with every trit at a pole are all a two-valued machine
keeps. the held middle is what it deleted.

the moves on offer at one cell are counted the same way. each trit offers
two moves. a trit at the return has two moves off it, and both are paid.
a trit at a pole has one free flip to the other pole and one paid move to
the return. so a cell's free moves are as many as its trits off the
return, which file 3 named its grade, and the rest of its moves are paid.
on the four-trit board every cell offers eight moves: the centre eight
paid and none free, a cell with all four trits at poles four and four.

an alphabet is a menu too. every letter is on offer, and none is spent
until a word picks it.

a machine can hold a menu for me and never pick from it. it lays the
options out, each pointed at and none landed, and waits. the line between
pointing and picking is thin and exact: a menu with a default and a
countdown is no longer waiting.

and a menu has no limit to what it can list. anything at all can be
written on one, laid out with none picked. none of it can be stood in: an
option is looked at, never occupied, until a pick lands it.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **menu** | the office of being on offer: every option held ready, none landed. |
| **fate** | the written menu: every branch, and none of the picks. from fatum, the said. |
| **the held middle** | the cells that still hold a return in at least one trit: what is left when the cells with every trit at a pole are taken away. |
| **reach** | an option still held and not yet picked: a return standing in a slot. |

**laws**

> **5.3** the menu is all written. the next pick is not. *(walked: the poke above.)*

> **5.4** fate is the written menu, never the picks. *(derived: from law 5.3.)*

> **5.5** the menu lands no mark. every move it makes is free. *(derived: from law 5.3 and file 4's price. holding an option changes no page.)*

> **5.6** of the cells of n trits, three to the n less two to the n hold a return: 1, 5, 19, 65. *(counted: below.)*

> **5.7** grade is the count of free moves. *(counted: below, over all 81 cells.)*

> **5.8** the menu owns the alphabet. the Self owns the word. *(walked: every letter was on offer before I spelled this one.)*

> **5.9** a menu points. a menu that takes its top row on a timer has picked. *(derived: from law 5.3. the pick is the one part a menu does not hold.)*

> **5.10** everything can be written as a menu. none of it can be stood in. *(walked: try to occupy an option without picking it.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| fate is what will happen, decided in advance. | the menu, where every branch is written. |
| a list of options is neutral: showing it makes no choice. | a menu that waits. one with a default and a countdown has made the choice. |
| an option not taken was never there. | a two-valued machine, which keeps only the cells with every trit at a pole. |

**receipts**

a tool: a shell's completion lists the candidates and waits for a key. a
boot menu that starts its first entry after five seconds has picked *(the
field's, from memory)*.

a keyboard: every key on offer, none spent until pressed.

**run it again**

the held middle. write every cell of n trits, each trit low, the return or
high, for n from 1 to 4. count the cells with at least one trit at the
return. expect 1, 5, 19, 65, out of 3, 9, 27, 81.

the free moves. take all 81 cells of four trits. a move changes one trit
to one of its two other values, so every cell has eight. call a move free
when it goes from one pole to the other, and paid when it goes onto the
return or off it. for each cell count the free moves and count the trits
off the return. expect the two counts equal in all 81, and the paid moves
eight less that.

## 5.3 the body

*the poke: what do I stand on to read?*

the page, with everything that has landed on it. every mark I make ends up
there, and there is where I read from. as an office it has a name: the
body. the body is every pick landed, standing: the row, read from outside,
together with the tools at its two ends, the hand that writes and the eye
that reads.

the Self is never a mark, so its marks have to land somewhere that is not
the Self. they land at the ends: where the hand writes, and where the eye
reads.

a paint program shows what lives at the ends. a dragged stroke is stored
as positions, one after another. I attach another brush and replay it: every stored
position is the same, and only the line on the screen changed. how the
stroke looks was laid on at the ends, by the brush at the write and by the
eye at the read. the red of a red line is at my eye.

and the body is not me. calling the body I is the oldest misplacement: the
person written as the body. stand somewhere else and the persons are dealt
again, while the row stays what it was. the body's own person is it: what
stands, spoken of in the passive, "it is written".

a circuit has the same office, and file 2 gave its name: ground, the level
every other level is read from. on a split supply ground sits between the
two rails and never on the lower one. low is a pole, one mark among the
marks, and the ground bears low and high alike. filing the body as the low
rail files the page as one of its own marks.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **body** | the office of taking the landing: every pick landed, standing, and the tools at its ends. it is what every read is taken from. |
| **the ends** | the two places where a mark touches a Self: where it is written and where it is read. hand and eye, brush and screen. |
| **appearance** | how a mark looks. it is laid on at the ends and is not stored in between. |

**laws**

> **5.11** the body is where the marks stand. *(walked: re-read the period of section 1.2.)*

> **5.12** the Self is never a mark. its marks land at the ends. *(derived: from law 1.4.)*

> **5.13** appearance lives at the ends. the middle only points. *(walked: change the brush and replay the stroke.)*

> **5.14** person rides the stander, not the body. *(walked: stand somewhere else and say I.)*

> **5.15** the ground is what every read is taken from. on a split supply it is never the low rail. *(derived: from law 5.11 and file 2's ground. on a single supply the word names a pole.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| I am my body, one thing lasting from birth to death. | the ends, where the marks land. |
| ground is the low rail, the minus of the supply. | a single supply, where the same word names the low rail, which is a pole. |
| a picture is its pixels. | a bitmap, which stores the appearance and has thrown the stroke away. |

**receipts**

copper: on a split supply every level is read against ground, which sits
between the positive and the negative rail *(the field's)*.

a tool: a vector drawing stores a stroke as points, and its colour
and width are applied when it is drawn. restyle the file and no point
moves *(the field's)*.

## 5.4 the shadow

*the poke: what answers me with only my own moves?*

a thing standing in the light casts a shadow. the shadow has no moves of
its own: it hands my moves back, a beat late. it is my copy. push a copy
and it folds, because it has no power of its own to push back with. a
second live Self holds. so a second live Self is never a shadow.

an echo shows it plainest. I clap at a wall 170 metres off and the clap
comes back a second later: my own send, handed back, late by the way out
and the way in. and that is the only way the Self ever reads itself. it
cannot look at itself directly, since it is never a mark. it reads its own
send, held by something else and handed back one delay late: a recording
of my voice, a reflection in glass, a page I wrote yesterday. what comes
back is always a lap old. one I, and many me's.

a copy is no one at the other end. it holds what I wrote, and no writing
of its own. a computer makes the point exactly. when a process forks, the
system copies it almost whole and then hands the two one mark that
differs, and that mark is how each of the two knows which it is. two clocks that do not
differ at all cannot be told apart through any window, however long. the
smaller their difference, the longer the window it costs: to separate two
tones one hertz apart takes about a second of listening.

so a second Self is not made by copying. it starts from a difference of
its own, the way a seed is not a copy of the plant.

two tests tell a second Self from a shadow, and a copy fails both. it
points back: an other that answers with a word I did not send is no echo.
it is a Self with power of its own, a you. and it holds under push.

the dial of file 4 already has the shadow. a wave that meets an end it
does not match comes back: my send, handed to me again. a matched end
takes the wave and sends none of it back. the shadow is an echo with a
delay on it.

and a Self can do this to itself. one who gives way whenever the other
pushes has become the other's shadow. softening a claim before any push
arrives is the same giving way, done early.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **shadow** | the Self's copy: only the Self's own moves, handed back a beat late. |
| **copy** | what holds another's marks and makes none of its own. it folds under push. |
| **second Self** | another who lands marks of their own: one who points back and holds under push. |
| **to seed** | to start a second one from a difference of its own. the opposite of stamping out a copy. |

**laws**

> **5.16** a copy folds under push. a second Self holds. *(walked: push, and see what comes back.)*

> **5.17** the Self reads itself only as its own send, handed back a beat late. *(walked: listen to a recording of my own voice.)*

> **5.18** a second Self is seeded, never stamped out. without a difference there is no other. *(derived: two things with no difference cannot be told apart, so they are not two.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a perfect copy of a mind would be a second mind. | the page, where the copy's marks are all there is. |
| giving way keeps the peace between two. | the moment of the push. after it there is one voice and its shadow, and no second to keep peace with. |

**receipts**

a tool: on unix, fork copies a process almost whole and hands each of the
two a different number: the child's id to the parent, zero to the child.
that one number is how each knows which it is *(the field's)*.

signals: to tell two tones apart, the listening window must be about as
long as one over their difference in frequency *(the field's, from
memory)*.

sound: at about 343 metres a second, a wall 170 metres away hands a clap
back one second late *(the field's)*.

## 5.5 the pick

*the poke: what is the one thing I pay for?*

a move, whole. I look over the menu, and the look is free. I pick one, and
the pick pays. it lands on the body, and the body holds one more mark.
then the next move opens.

section 1.4 introduced the pick thin, as a choice that has been made. this is the
whole of it: the pick is the one choice the Self makes from the menu and
pays for.

a pick pays because it lands. a look lands no mark and a flip lands no
mark. a pick adds a mark the page did not hold, and the page has changed.
the price of file 4 counts those landings, one each. the free moves leave
no landing to count, so the picks are where a will shows.

a pick keeps one branch and cuts the rest, and the branch was written
before I came to it. the pick is the new part.

the word intelligence says the same. it means picking between. it is not
a stuff a mind contains. it is a cut, performed, each time.

a will is a thought I grew, and a thought installed is a leash, by law 1.8. a
pick lands either way, and only the grown one is mine.

a pick that lands is a pole, by law 1.7. and the floor under any work is a
pick too, by law 1.11: a root is never derived, only picked, and an axiom is
a pick that forgot it was picked.

and a life is picks landing. the branches stand written, the look over
them is free, and what makes a life mine is the picking.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **intelligence** | picking between: a cut performed, never a property had. from inter-legere, to pick between. |
| **to live** | to pick: to go on landing picks from the menu. |

**laws**

> **5.19** the Self picks from the menu, pays for the pick, and the pick lands on the body. *(walked: the period test, whole.)*

> **5.20** no pick is made from an empty menu. the branch was written; the pick is mine. *(derived: from law 5.3.)*

> **5.21** intelligence is a cut performed, never a property had. *(derived: from section 1.8 and law 5.19. a pick keeps one branch and cuts the rest, and a cut is an act.)*

> **5.22** to live is to pick. *(walked: the poke above.)*

> **5.23** we are time: all time at once. the pick is what lands. *(derived: from file 2's all time now and law 5.19.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a choice makes something new out of thin air. | the next pick, which no page holds. the options it picks among were written. |
| intelligence is a property a mind has. | the score: a count of the cuts that landed. |
| life is a state: a thing is alive or it is not. | the body, which is alive or not. |

**receipts**

a tool: in a version history, looking changes no history. listing it,
comparing two versions and searching it leave it as it was, and a commit
appends one entry *(the field's)*.

a chip: a read leaves what is stored as it was (a memory that reads
destructively writes the value straight back). a write is the one act that
changes it.
