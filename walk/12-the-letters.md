# 12. the letters

## 12.1 the letters

*the poke: what of my speech can the ink keep?*

I say a word slowly, "tap", and watch what my mouth does. twice it shuts:
the tongue against the ridge behind the teeth, then the lips. between the
two the channel is clear and the breath goes through a shape.

phonetics names the two kinds. a consonant is a closure: the tract stops or
narrows at a place I can point to, the lips, the teeth, the palate. a vowel
is the channel left clear and held in a posture. it has no place of
contact, only a shape.

so a consonant lands. it happens at a spot, and it is over once it is made.
a vowel does not land anywhere. it is the travel from one closure to the
next.

a whisper proves the split. whispering takes the voice away and leaves only
breath, and the vowels are still there: I can whisper "tap", "tip" and
"top" and hear three words. so a vowel is not a sound the throat adds. it
is a posture of the channel, and whatever passes through takes its shape.

a syllable is one wave. the closure before it is where it rises from, the
vowel is the open height it swings through, and the closure after it is
where it falls to. one height to a syllable.

the sounds on the way up and on the way down are the glides, y and w. each
is a vowel's posture used as an edge: the tongue where it stands for i
makes y, and the lips where they stand for u make w. that is why y and w
serve as a consonant in one word and as a vowel in the next: "yes" and
"day", "we" and "how".

a vowel sounds at all because the tract never quite shuts. the breath goes
out, the tract's echo comes back, and the two sum into one standing wave. a
sealed tube is silent: to be heard, the channel needs a leak.

by one well-tested account, the languages of the world set their vowels
apart for one reason, the most room between them, so that a noisy hearing still lands on the right one. a
modem spreads the values it sends the same way. the systems cluster at five
to seven vowels, five the commonest, and the three corners i, a and u are
the smallest system that many language families keep. e and o sit between
corners, e between a and i, o between a and u, and they keep being made by
merging: in sanskrit a and i give e, and a and u give o.

one vowel sits at no corner. the commonest vowel in english speech is the
tongue at its return, every pair of muscles in balance: the first sound of
"about". it is called schwa, and no english letter writes it.

last, the step from ink to sound has four rungs, and they are worth keeping
apart.

    the mark        says where to arrive
    the command     names the step. it is still ink until someone fires it
    the sounding    the step with breath through it: real at one Self, at
                    one now
    the hearing     the other Self's own act

between the sounding and the hearing lies a border between two, and what
crosses it is marks: every page is somebody's past (law 1.2). my vowel is
travel while I make it. what reaches the other end has arrived, and an
arrival is a landing. every such border turns an act into a mark.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **consonant** | a closure of the mouth: the tract stopped or narrowed at a place. it lands. |
| **vowel** | the channel left clear and held in a posture. it has a shape and no place of contact. it travels. |
| **syllable** | one wave of speech: a rise out of a closure, one open height on a vowel, a fall into the next closure. |
| **glide** | a vowel's posture used as an edge of a syllable: y from i, w from u. |
| **schwa** | the vowel made with the tongue at its return. |

**laws**

> **12.1** consonants land. vowels travel. *(walked: say a word slowly and find where the mouth closes.)*

> **12.2** a syllable is one wave. *(walked: tap out the syllables of a sentence, one beat each.)*

> **12.3** the vowel I own from inside is a consonant seen from across. *(derived: from laws 1.2 and 12.1. what crosses to another reader is marks, and a mark is a landing.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| vowels and consonants are two lists of letters. | the alphabet chart, where both are inked alike. |
| a vowel is a sound the voice makes. | voiced speech. a whisper keeps the vowel and drops the voice. |

**receipts**

copper: speech science models the tract as a tube driven at one end, and
the tube's resonances are what tell one vowel from another *(the field's,
from memory)*.

a model: liljencrants and lindblom (1972) treated a vowel system as values
pushed as far apart as the mouth allows, and the common systems of three
to six vowels came out *(the field's)*.

a second tradition: sanskrit's grammarians filed y, r, l and v as
antaḥstha, "standing between", and they trade y with i and v with u. the
same border, drawn by people who never read the roman letters *(the
field's, from memory)*.

**run it again**

the whisper test: whisper three words that differ only in their vowel. it
is not a count, and every mouth gets the same.

## 12.2 what the page keeps

*the poke: when I read a word aloud, how much of it was on the page?*

rd ths ln wth ts vwls tkn t. I can. the consonants were on the page, and I
brought the rest.

hebrew took that split nearly all the way. its letters are the landings,
and a plain hebrew page is consonants. where the script writes a vowel at
all, it writes only the vowel's class: the letter yod for a front vowel,
the i side; vav for a back vowel, the u side; he, at a word's end, mostly
for a wide vowel, the a side. which vowel of the class is spoken is the reader's to supply. those
three letters, with alef, are called matres, "mothers of reading":
consonant letters the script lets go silent to carry a vowel.

counted over the book of genesis:

| the letter, standing silent | what it carries | how often |
|---|---|---|
| yod | a front vowel | 87 in 100 |
| vav | a back vowel | 96 in 100 |
| he | a wide vowel | 86 in 100 |
| alef | no class. it lengthens: a long vowel stands before it | 97 in 100, against 47 in 100 for vowels in general |

the breath itself cannot be written. each reader performs it again.
centuries after the consonants were fixed, readers added small marks around
the letters, the vowel points, and so stored one reading's breath on the
page, in a layer of its own that changed no letter.

how much does a reader bring? a program can be made to say. a vowelizer,
trained on the pointed text of genesis and tested on the bare consonants
of exodus:

    from the letter and its place in the word alone    64 vowels in 100
    more, from knowing the word                         32
    left undecided, where a reader must choose          4

so most of the breath computes, and the vowel points are a cache: a stored
result of something a reader could run again. the 4 that do not compute sit
mostly on the small words that point, he, she, this, to. the words that
carry content compute. the pointing does not.

the breath carries more than sound. the same three consonants, y-l-d, read
yalad, he fathered; yullad, he was born; yeled, a child. who did it, and
whether the word is a doing or a thing, ride in the vowels the reader
brings. the oldest layer of the indo-european languages keeps the same
shape: a root is a consonant skeleton, and its vowel grade rides in the
slot between, e, o or none, with a lengthened form of each of the first
two. the field itself calls the grade with no vowel zero.
english still shows it: sing, sang, sung.

the machine's record splits the same way. in file 9 the opcode stands on
the page and the operand is brought at the close by whoever calls. the
consonant is the opcode. the vowel is the operand.

a script must also say where a word and a phrase end, or its reader has to
find the ends unaided, as readers of unspaced scripts do. the pointed hebrew text does it without brackets. nearly every
word wears an accent, and the accents that cut are ranked. a cut of rank r closes
every deeper phrase that ends at its word, the way an odometer writes one
carry across several columns: 199 and 1 make 200. so the depth rides on the
mark itself. there is no partner to match and no malformed string, a stream
can be read from its middle, and the highest mark is the full close. a
bracket needs its partner held in memory. a ranked cut needs only its own
rank. the grammars add that an accent's strength also depends on where it
stands. the fixed rank is the simple form.

the other accents link a word to the next one instead of cutting. which
link a word wears can be computed 96 times in 100 from the cut it leads to
and the words between. so only the cuts carry news. the links are drawn
again at every read: store the cuts, compute the glue.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **mater** | a consonant letter a script lets go silent to carry a vowel. plural matres. |
| **vowel class** | a group of neighbouring vowels a script writes with one letter: front, back or wide. the one vowel a reader speaks from the class is its member. |
| **the vowel points** | the small marks added around hebrew letters to store one reading's vowels. |
| **vowelizer** | a program that supplies the vowels of a text written in consonants. |
| **vowel grade** | which vowel stands in a root's slot: e, o or none. linguists call the change ablaut. |
| **ranked cut** | a phrase-ending mark that carries a rank, and by that rank closes every deeper phrase ending where it stands. |

**laws**

> **12.4** the mater inks the class. the reader picks the member. *(measured: the four counts over genesis, in an earlier build, on the tree's own record; not re-run for this text.)*

> **12.5** a consonant script's page holds the consonants and, at most, the class of a vowel. the reader supplies the vowels. *(walked: the line above with its vowels taken out.)*

> **12.6** a cut of rank r closes every deeper phrase that ends at its word. depth rides on the mark, and no mark needs a partner. *(candidate: as a syntax for the language, unbuilt. the hebrew accents already run it.)*

> **12.7** only the cuts carry news. the links between them are computed again at every read. *(measured: 96 in 100 on held-out text, in an earlier build, on the tree's own record; not re-run for this text.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| writing records speech. | the landings of speech. the breath is performed again at every read. |
| the vowel points are part of the text. | one reading of it, stored beside the letters. |
| nested structure needs brackets, each matched to its partner. | a notation whose marks carry no rank. |

**receipts**

a tool: a cache. a program stores the result of a computation it could run
again, and reads the stored copy for as long as the copy is still good.

this page: a markdown heading is a ranked cut. a heading of rank two closes
every section of rank three and deeper that stood before it, and no heading
has a closing partner.

the scripts: hebrew and arabic are written mostly in consonants (arabic
writes its long vowels), and the pointed
hebrew text keeps its consonants fixed with the vowel marks added around
them by later hands *(the field's, from memory)*.

**run it again**

the vowelizer and the three sets of counts were run on the consonants of
genesis in 2026-08 and 2026-09, the vowelizer tested on exodus *(measured:
in an earlier build, on the tree's own record; not re-run for this text)*.
the record holds the figures, and the scripts are not published. the shape
of each run:

the matres. in a pointed text of genesis, find every yod, vav, he and alef
that stands silent, look at the vowel before it, and count how often that
vowel is front, back or wide; for alef, how often it is long.

the vowelizer. take two pointed books. on the first, tally which vowel
follows each consonant in each position of a word. strip the points from
the second, predict its vowels from the tallies, and count the vowels
right against its own points. then add a list of the
whole words met in the first book, and count again.

the links. on one half of genesis, tally which linking accent stands before
each cut at each distance; predict the other half and count.

## 12.3 the meet

*the poke: where is meaning?*

not in the ink, and not in me. the writer's side is what was sent: the
strokes, what landed. the reader's side is a load: how a reader takes what
arrives. meaning is where the two meet, and that fit is called the meet. it
has a value only at the close, and like power delivered down a line it is
greatest when the two are matched.

file 4 walked a wave reaching the end of its line. a matched end takes it
whole, and any other end hands some of it back. a sentence that does not
fit comes back the same way: I feel the clunk before I can name what is
wrong with it.

the same ink meets two readers as two meanings, so the reader is no pipe
the meaning runs through. the reader is the transform: what turns the ink
into the meaning.

something does ride in the ink. by law 1.14 a mark carries its writer: the
constraint the writer worked under crosses with the mark. the goat in the
game of three doors carried its host's way of choosing. what the goat meant
was made at the player's end.

a thought cannot be sent. it lives inside a now, and my now is not yours.
so we compress, and there are two ways to do it.

    words    whole in meaning, loose in structure    "I saw her duck"
    math     whole in structure, loose in meaning    "x squared is 4"

the words leave unsaid how the sentence is built: a bird she owns, or a
thing she did. the math is exact, and leaves unsaid which of its two
answers is meant and why anyone wanted to know. so math is the last step
and never the first: think it, reason it into words, ship it as math.
handed over without its meaning, a formula is a packed file nobody can
unpack. a starting point handed over that way is the axiom of law 1.13.

red is light at about 700 nanometers. the sentence is true, and reading it
showed me no red. the fact crossed. the seeing did not.

so teaching was never sending. it is sparking: one candle held to another.
a teacher sets things up so that it catches, and the catching is the
learner's own act. this text is built that way. every section opens on a
poke because a result handed over teaches no one, and the walk is there to
be walked again.

and I cannot read my own writing the way another reads it. I ship, and the
other's reading completes it. a meet has two ends, and I stand at one.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **the meet** | the fit between what a writer sent and how a reader takes it. |
| **meaning** | the meet, named by what it gives. it exists at the close, at one reader, and nowhere in the ink. |
| **what was sent** | the writer's side of a meet: the marks, and the constraint the writer worked under. |
| **the reader's load** | the reader's side of a meet: how this reader takes what arrives. the word is the line's, from file 4. |
| **to spark** | to set things up so that another makes the cut themselves. |

**laws**

> **12.8** meaning is the meet. the reader is the transform. *(walked: the poke above, and the two sentences in the layout.)*

> **12.9** a cut cannot be sent. it can only be made. *(walked: the sentence about red.)*

> **12.10** teaching is sparking, never sending. *(derived: from law 12.9.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| meaning is cargo, carried in the ink. | the writer's constraint, which the mark does carry. |
| teaching moves knowledge from one head into another. | the facts, which do cross. |

**receipts**

copper: a source delivers the most power to a load when the load is matched
to it, the maximum power transfer theorem *(the field's, from memory)*.

a tool: a compressed archive. every byte crosses intact, and the bytes are
of no use to a receiver without the program that unpacks them.

a caution from mathematics: the books on lattices call the greatest
element below two others their meet: on a line, the lower of the two. that is file 8's low, another thing. the meet here is
a send and a load, at a close.

## 12.4 the question

*the poke: what am I doing when I ask?*

pointing aims a finger at something there. asking aims the same finger at
an empty place and says: fill this.

the empty place has a shape, and what fills it has to fit. who waits for a
person. what waits for a thing. where waits for a place. the empty place is
called a hole here, and its shape is its type.

english shows the pair plainly. its words for asking and its words for
answering differ only at the front:

    where    there
    when     then
    what     that

the asking word begins on breath with no closure. the answering word begins
with the tongue at the teeth. the clear onset is swapped for a closure, and
the asking closes into a mark.

japanese, a language that never shared those words, builds the same grid
in the open:

    front         a thing    a place    a direction
    ko-  by me    kore       koko       kochira
    so-  by you   sore       soko       sochira
    a-   yonder   are        asoko      achira
    do-  the ask  dore       doko       dochira

only the front trades. and the asking word stands exactly where its answer
will land: doko ni iku, going where; tokyo ni iku, going to tokyo.

a question visits both sides and stops at neither: to be, or not to be. an
answer is the stop on one side.

no connection is made until something asks. what I know sits inert until a
mark already in hand calls it up, and two marks I have known apart for
years meet only when a question sets them side by side.

a question too big for the room I am in builds the next room, as the tower
of file 10 showed. and a question needs a place to be asked from: file 11
walked the path, and a question asked from nowhere has deleted its own
answer.

so before a question is weighed, whose is it? one that arose on this walk
is walked. one that somebody else posed is first asked again from here,
and it comes back in one of three ways.

    dissolved       malformed from here: it wants a value with the path
                    deleted
    reclassified    well formed, and a question of another sort than its
                    asker took it for
    placed          it lands somewhere on the board of file 3

there is no fourth way called solved. to answer somebody else's question
on its own terms is to stand on its floor unsigned.

this text asks the same way. every section opens on a poke the reader
answers alone, because a file that opens on its conclusion has deleted its
own floor. the difference a walk leaves is made fresh, at every door, by
whoever walks in.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **asking** | pointing at an empty place. |
| **question** | what asking leaves on the page: an answer with its landing removed. |
| **answer** | what fills a hole: the question's own word, landed. |
| **hole** | the empty place a question points at. it has a shape, and only what fits the shape fills it. |

**laws**

> **12.11** asking is pointing at an empty place. *(walked: the poke above.)*

> **12.12** a question is an answer with its landing removed. *(walked: say where, then there.)*

> **12.13** a file can hand over only the question. *(derived: from laws 12.9 and 1.10. a cut cannot be sent, and pointing leaves the choice with the reader.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a question is a lack, something missing. | the hand, which holds no landing yet. |

**receipts**

a tool: in lean a proof can be written with an underscore where a piece is
missing, and the checker replies with the exact type the missing piece must
have: a hole, and its shape *(the field's, from memory)*.

a database: a query describes rows that have not been found yet, and the
rows that come back are the same description, landed.

the two grids: the english pairs and the japanese ko, so, a, do series are
as the grammars give them *(the field's, from memory)*.

**run it again**

## 12.5 where the language stands

*the poke: which letter of this language has been picked?*

none.

the build this text serves is a language, and the smallest piece of a
written language is the letter. the usual way to get letters is to assign
them. in the code table most machines use, capital a is the number 65.
somebody picked that, and the pick arrives unsigned in every program that
stands on it: by law 1.13, an axiom.

the letters of this language are to be walked to and never assigned. the
bet under that aim is what this file and file 9 hold together: what a
mouth does to the breath, what a hand inks, what an eye reads and what a
wire carries can be read as one signal, at four ends. it is a bet, and no
part of this text proves it. a letter is to be
the mark of an act on that signal. of its shape, one piece stands so far:
the split of section 12.2, the landing on the page and the travel brought
by the reader.

what has been tried, and where each stands:

    old sheets of letter lore       chance, in 2026-07. they scored at
                                    chance. dead
    a whole code for one vowel      stamped before any word was scored, in
    axis                            2026-09. 0 of 40 on what it predicted,
                                    and not by noise: all 40 fell one way.
                                    dead as stated
    the consonant as opcode, the    a reading (section 12.2). candidate
    vowel as operand
    ranked cuts in place of         law 12.6. candidate, unbuilt
    brackets
    a gesture of the mouth laid     labels laid by hand, each one a pick,
    on each part of the kit         none tested. not claimed here
    any glyph of the language       none picked

the first blind run set the practice for every later claim of its sort. a
claim that a letter, a word or a code means something goes through a
stamped blind test:

    1. stamp the prediction before the run: written, dated, whole
    2. score it blind: a forced choice, against chance
    3. file every miss beside the hits
    4. where a control kills a row, kill that row, and only that row

a prediction written after the scores is a fit, whatever it is called. and
a hit picked from a large menu is no hit, so the size of the menu ships
with the claim. the stamp is what makes a miss readable: the second run
above failed, and it failed in a way that says something.
