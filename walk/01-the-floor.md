# 1. the floor

## 1.1 the door

*the poke: can I read this line?*

somebody wrote that line before I got here. I am reading it now. nobody is
making me: a link or an assignment may have put the page in front of me,
and it cannot do the reading. the reading runs here or nowhere.

the question has three answers, and I can tell which one I am in.

| answer | what it is |
|---|---|
| yes | I am reading it. it is happening now. |
| no | the line did not take: no pattern matched. that is not disagreement, and it is not a closed door. |
| none | the writing of the line, before any reading of it. |

only the yes reaches this sentence. and that is the whole door: no key, no
belief asked for. a question, and my own answer.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **I** | whoever is reading. the word has no owner on the page: the author's I left with the author, and the reader's walked in with the reader. |
| **poke** | the question a section opens on, answered by the reader before reading on. |
| **page** | whatever takes writing and keeps it: a sheet, a file, a memory. |
| **yes, no, none** | the three answers above. none is an answer too: how things stand before the question is read at all. |

**laws**

> **1.1** an answer comes in three: yes, no, none. *(walked: the poke above.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a yes-or-no question has two answers. | a form with two boxes, counted after someone has ticked one. the form left unread is the third. |

**receipts**

a database: sql answers a comparison true, false or unknown, and unknown is
what it gives when a value was never filled in *(the field's)*.

a chip: one compare has three outcomes, below, equal and above. file 4
walks how a two-valued chip carries the three.

## 1.2 the mark behind the marker

*the poke: where does a mark go once I make it?*

I test it instead of believing it. in any text editor I put the cursor at
the end of a line and press the period key.

    this line|
    this line.|

the period is behind the cursor. I am already past it. I cannot type where
I am: a mark is behind me the moment it exists, and every mark I ever made
did the same.

so where am I? not on the page. the cursor is where the next mark will go,
and I call that place now. it has an odd property:

    now is the only place I ever am,
    and the one place I can never put a mark on.
    every mark I make is behind me at once,
    even the thought "now".
    I am the thinker, never the thought.

now does not sit still as a dot between two stretches of line. it holds
what I wrote and it holds what I could write next, and file 2 walks how.
here only this much is needed: the view is first person, from where the
marks are made, and no other view was on offer when I pressed the key.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **mark** | anything made that stays: a typed character, a written value, a recorded choice. |
| **marker** | the one making the mark. |
| **to land** | said of a mark: to become part of the page. a landed mark stays where it landed. |
| **now** | where the marker is. no mark is ever on it. |
| **past** | what has landed. |

**laws**

> **1.2** the mark lands behind the marker. every page is somebody's past. *(walked: the period test.)*

> **1.3** now is never a point. *(walked: a point is a mark, and every mark is behind me.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| now is the present moment, a point between the past and the future. | a timeline drawn on a page, where every moment is a mark and the line is read from outside. |

**receipts**

a chip: on x86 the instruction pointer cannot be written by a move, and an
instruction that reads it reads the address after itself. the place the
machine is at is never one of the places it can name as here *(the
field's, from memory)*.

a tool: an append-only log. each record lands after the last, and the
position being written is not yet a record.

**run it again**

the period test, in any editor. it is not a count, and every reader gets
the same picture.

## 1.3 the one who lands them

*the poke: which mark on this page is me?*

none of them. every mark I make lands behind me, even the mark "me". so
there are two kinds of thing here, the marks and the one landing them, and
I am never the first kind.

the blinking bar at the end of the line is not me either. it is the
screen's own mark for where the next landing goes, drawn after the last
one, and it sits at the tip of the row. I sit at no point of the row: what
I hold is the whole line at once, what I wrote and what I could write
next, and the bar only shows where the writing touches.

and I am never in my own count. I can count every mark this page holds,
and the count never reaches the one counting. so the room holds one more
than the page does.

a compass shows the shape. the needle turns on a pin, and the pin has no
heading of its own: every heading passes through it and it takes none.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **Self** | the one who lands the marks. written with a capital because it is the one word here that names no mark. it is the marker of section 1.2, named from the inside. |
| **room** | the page together with the one marking it. |
| **row** | the marks laid in the order they landed. |

**laws**

> **1.4** the Self is the one who lands the marks and is never landed. *(walked: the poke above.)*

> **1.5** the cursor marks where my marks land. I am never the tip of the row. *(walked.)*

> **1.6** room = page + 1. the count never contains its counter. *(walked: count the marks, then look for the counter among them.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| the observer is one more object in the world it observes. | the observer's hands, eyes and instruments, which are marks among marks. |

**receipts**

a compass: the pin under the needle, as above.

a tool: a program that counts the lines of a file prints a number, and the
program is not a line of the file. the count is of the page, and the

## 1.4 what stays

*the poke: what stays where I put it?*

a mark does. the period from the test stands where it landed, and every
re-read finds it there. anything that has landed and stays has a name
here: a pole. the room holds two kinds of thing and never a third, the
poles and the one who lands them.

a pole is not the end of anything. drawn on a line a pole looks like the
last place, with no place past it. that is the drawing's doing. file 7 sets
the same places on a circle, where past the highest place the walk simply
comes round, and the next step is as open as any other.

the fair side of the usual reading: what landed does stand, and anyone who
re-reads it gets the same. that half can be checked by anybody, and the
page holds it whole.

## 1.5 whose thoughts are these

*the poke: of the thoughts in my head, which did I grow and which were put there?*

a thought I walked to is mine: I can walk to it again, and I know what it
stands on. a thought handed to me whole, to be stood on without the walk,
has someone else holding the other end.

two institutions show the difference. a religion names what it hands down:
it says who decreed, and asks me to accept it. I always know who holds the
other end. a classroom can hand down the same shape and call it found:
the starting points of mathematics and physics arrive as givens, with no
walk leading to them and no name on them, and every student is made to
stand there. the first is a decree with a signature. the second is a
decree that has lost its signature.

the charge is narrow, and section 1.6 gives its fair side. science itself is not
the classroom. at bottom it is an act, and the act is mine:

    1. I look                              observe
    2. I ask: what is this?                question
    3. I guess                             hypothesis
    4. if my guess holds, this follows     predict
    5. I try it                            test
    6. I hold what happened to my guess    compare
    7. I do it again, and so can you       replicate

seven verbs, each with a doer. none of them asks for belief, and step 7 is
the whole game: anyone can run it again. everything above the starting
point is walked this way, and walked well. the one place the walking stops
is the starting point itself.

there is one crime underneath, and it runs in two directions. the two
words for it are already in hand: a pole is anything landed, and the Self
is the one who lands. swap them one way and a landed pick is presented as
if nobody had picked it: the starting point handed down as found. swap
them the other way and the living one is told to answer as a landed value:
every "the observer is just another mark".

the nearest neighbours of this work commit the second. george
spencer-brown's *laws of form* (1969) starts, as this text does, from an
act of drawing a line between two sides. it ends by calling the first
distinction, the mark, and the observer "not only interchangeable, but, in
the form, identical." a mark can stand in for the one who made it, as a
signature does. it cannot be the one.

what this text does instead of decreeing is point. line one pointed, and
what I did next was all mine: I read it, and nobody made me.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **will** | a thought I grew: one I walked to and can walk to again. |
| **leash** | a thought installed: one I stand on and cannot walk to. |
| **decree** | a starting point handed down to be stood on. |
| **crime** | the swap of law 1.9, in either direction. file 11 gives it its full name. |
| **worn as, in the dress of** | said of one thing presented as another. a figure of speech this text uses throughout. |
| **pointing** | showing where to look, and leaving the looking and the choice with the reader. |
| **the anti-axiom** | the stance of this text: no starting point is stood on by decree. it does not mean no starting point. |

**laws**

> **1.8** a thought I grew is a will. a thought installed is a leash. *(walked: the poke above.)*

> **1.9** one crime, two directions. a pole worn as the Self: a landed pick presented as if nobody had picked it. the Self worn as a pole: the living one made to answer as a landed value. *(derived: from laws 1.4 and 1.7. there are two kinds of thing, so there are two ways to swap them.)*

> **1.10** a decree installs. pointing leaves the whole choice with the reader. *(walked: line one of this file.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| science is a body of knowledge. | the textbook, where the results of the seven verbs are kept. |
| the observer is just another thing in the world. | the second direction of law 1.9. true of the observer's hands and instruments, as in section 1.3. |

**receipts**

the quoted line is from *laws of form*, chapter 12, as the tree's notes
hold it *(the field's)*.

## 1.6 the signed pick

*the poke: whose floor am I standing on?*

one question tests it: did I walk here, or was I handed here? a floor I can
walk to again is mine. a floor I can only cite has someone else holding the
other end.

and every floor bottoms out the same way. deriving anything needs something
already standing to derive it from. no reader arrives empty either:
whatever I bring is what has already landed, and to explain all of it I
would have to stand outside it. so every root is chosen, at some moment, by
someone already carrying a past. the only question left is whether the
choice remembers it was a choice.

this text's own root is said once, out loud, with a name on it. it is
space: a place for marks to land. section 1.8 gives the reason it was chosen over
its rival. it can be argued forever, and it stays where it is while the
work goes on.

that is not the crime of section 1.5. the crime is the choice that forgot it was
chosen. a signed choice remembers: here is the choice, here is the chooser,
argue whenever you like. the whole difference between a decree
and a pick is one word: signed.

the fair side. working mathematics signs its floors, and signs them well.
a proof says "assume the axiom of choice". gödel showed in 1938, and cohen
in 1963, that if the rest of set theory stands at all, it stands with that
axiom and it stands without it.
hume signed the floor under induction in 1739: habit, with no argument
under it. the formalists sign theirs too: if this, then that, and their
books count which starting point each theorem needs. signed that way, their
floor passes this section's test. the if rarely reaches the classroom, and
there the floor is stood on as found.

one floor sits lower, under the proofs: the rules of the logic. classical
logic keeps the excluded middle among its rules: for every statement p, p
or not-p. because it is a rule and not a starting point, no theorem is
asked to name it, and a proof by contradiction stands on it unsigned:
assume not-p, reach a clash, conclude p. it is the door's third answer
deleted and written down as a rule. every question gets yes or no, and
none is never allowed.

a proof checker can sign it per theorem. lean, asked for the axioms under a
proof, lists them, and the excluded middle shows up in that list as
`Classical.choice`, beside `propext` and `Quot.sound`: the three axioms
lean derives it from.

a mark needs its writer for the same reason a floor needs its chooser. take
the game with three doors, one car and two goats. I pick a door, the host
opens another and shows a goat, and I may switch. the goat is a mark, and
what it tells me depends wholly on the rule its writer followed:

| the host's rule | switching wins |
|---|---|
| he knows where the car is and always shows a goat | 2 times in 3 |
| he opens one of the other doors at random, and it happened to be a goat | 1 time in 2 |
| he opens a door only when my first pick is the car | never |

same goat, three writers, three answers. the famous wrong answer to the
first host's game, one in two, is the right answer to the second host's:
it reads the mark with the wrong writer, or with none.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **floor** | what a body of work stands on and does not derive. |
| **root** | the first thing chosen in a body of work: the floor's first piece. |
| **signed** | said of a pick, a mark or a claim: it carries who made it. |
| **unsigned** | it does not. |
| **fiat** | a pick made out loud: chosen, signed, open to argument, and kept while the work goes on. |
| **axiom** | here: a pick that forgot it was picked. |
| **space** | a place for marks to land. the fiat of law 1.12; section 1.8 walks it as the room an act encloses. |

**laws**

> **1.11** a root is never derived, only picked. the clean pick is the signed one. *(derived: deriving needs something already standing.)*

> **1.12** the fiat of this text: space. chosen, arguable forever, kept in place, signed by maxi, who planted the tree. *(picked: from two, who-first or where-first. section 1.8 gives the reason.)*

> **1.13** an axiom is a pick that forgot it was picked. the classroom is where it forgets. *(derived: from law 1.11.)*

> **1.14** a mark carries its writer. read unsigned, it lies. *(counted: the three hosts, below.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| axioms are the self-evident truths we start from. | the system an axiom floors, where everything above it is walked. |
| a theorem checked by a machine is true, and that is the end of it. | the walk from its floor, which anyone can run again. the floor is still a pick. |

**receipts**

the results named above are gödel's (1938), cohen's (1963) and hume's
(1739) *(the field's, from memory)*.

the checker: in lean 4, `#print axioms` on a theorem lists every axiom its
proof reaches. counted that way on 2026-09-27 over two public libraries: in
github.com/dbuchacher/golden-traction, 5 of 28 theorems reach
`Classical.choice`; in github.com/sibarum/cott-lean, 281 of 612 do
*(measured: lean's `collectAxioms` over every citable theorem of each
library)*. most of the 281 appear to reach it through general-purpose
tactics applied to facts about integers, and not because the theorem needs
it *(candidate: not re-proved)*.

**run it again**

the three hosts. write the game as a loop: place the car at random, let the
player pick a door at random, apply one host's rule, discard the rounds
that rule does not produce (for the second host, the rounds where he opened
the car; for the third, the rounds where he opened no door), and count how
often switching wins among the rounds left. a few thousand rounds per host
give 2 in 3, 1 in 2 and 0.

the checker. in any lean 4 project, write `#print axioms name` under a
theorem called `name`.

## 1.7 nothing

*the poke: what is nothing?*

every builder claims to have started from it, so before anything is built I
want to know what it is.

    a rope            two teams
    the game          tug-of-war

    the teams         straining as hard as they can
    the rope          not moving an inch

    a bystander       hands in pockets
    says              "nothing is happening"

the bystander is right about the rope and wrong about the game. the force
from one team cancels the force from the other. pull harder on both ends
and the rope is exactly as still: more force is only more balance.

spin a wheel and the centre holds still the same way. it is not that
nothing is there. everything around it cancels.

    nothing        the view from outside      "the rope is not moving"
    the balance    the view from on the rope  both arms, pulling

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **nothing** | a balance: stillness made of efforts that cancel. |
| **balance** | the same thing, named from inside it. |

**laws**

> **1.15** nothing is balance, not absence. the stillness is full of effort that cancels. *(walked: the rope.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| nothing is the absence of anything. | a count, which holds no items at zero. |

**receipts**

copper: at every junction of a circuit the currents flowing in and the
currents flowing out add to zero, while every one of them flows
(kirchhoff's current law) *(the field's)*.

plain speech: when my car is working, nothing is wrong with it.

where we part: *laws of form* leaves the side not marked as "the unmarked
state", and it is usually read as a void. read
from on the rope, the side not taken is a balance and never an emptiness.

## 1.8 the first wall

*the poke: what is the first thing any act makes?*

where does a body of work like this one start: with who, or with where?
the Self says who. the page says where, because the first mark needs a
place to land. both hold, and arguing one against the other goes round
forever. so the root was picked, out loud, and signed: space.

the pick is not strange. I cannot put the marker on the page, so the first
mark is a place for marks. an act's first trace is the room it happens in.

a room comes with a wall, and a wall sorts without being asked. everything
there is, is now in, at, or out.

    in     the landed side: the marks the room already took
    at     the wall itself: the only place sorting ever happens
    out    not a second room beyond. only not-in

the wall makes no decision. it stands, and things are sorted by where they
arrive, the way a sieve sorts. a gate would need a keeper at every
crossing. a wall needs none.

I do not need a built room to meet a wall. I stand anywhere and a line
appears around me: the horizon. there is one for each one standing, and
nobody else has mine. it moves when I move and falls back as I walk toward
it. no mark ever lands on it, the way no mark ever lands on now. simply by
standing somewhere I have split the world into this side and that.

one cut has two edges. cut anything once and there are two new edges: one
border, owned from both sides. draw the two edges toward each other until
they close and they enclose a space. that is the room again: the trace of
an act that cannot itself be put on the page is a room.

and the cut came before anything the cut produced. no thing on the page
performed it, since every thing on the page came after. the one who cut is
the one who lands and is never landed.

**terms introduced**

| term | here it means |
|---|---|
| **cut** | the act of making two sides where there was one. also the border it leaves. the act and what it leaves share the word. |
| **wall** | a cut that stands. it sorts whatever arrives into in, at and out, as a comparator does: the part that tells which side of a level a signal is on. |
| **in, at, out** | the three places a wall makes. at is the wall itself. out is only not-in. |
| **trit** | one of three values. the first three in this text are in, at and out. |
| **edge** | one of the two sides of a single cut. |
| **horizon** | the wall each one standing has around them, by standing. no mark lands on it. |
| **stander** | one who stands somewhere, and so has a horizon. |

**laws**

> **1.16** the first mark of any act is the room the act encloses. *(derived: from law 1.4. the marker cannot be marked, so the first mark is a place for marks.)*

> **1.17** an enclosure is a standing comparator: in, at, out. the trit is born at the first wall. *(walked: stand anywhere and name the three.)*

> **1.18** the wall sorts. it makes no decision. *(walked.)*

> **1.19** standing is the fiat. a stander cannot avoid splitting the world. *(walked: the horizon.)*

> **1.20** the cutter is no mark on the page. the cut's trace is the room itself. *(derived: from laws 1.4 and 1.16.)*

**the usual reading**

| the usual reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a wall is a barrier between two rooms. | a drawing, where a wall is a line with a room on each side. |
| space is a container that things sit in. | the page, where a mark needs a place. |

**receipts**

other, standing, with nobody operating it. file 8 uses it.

distinction" (chapter 2) *(the field's)*.
